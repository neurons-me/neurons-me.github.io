To connect two digital islands, the system doesn't need to consult a central database. It computes the exact space where both coexist.

Two islands. One shared space.

Each identity keeps its own context, relationships, and rules. The connection appears only when a computable match exists between the two spaces.

The result isn't a full copy of A or B. It's a third space: the minimal, verifiable set that can exist between the two.

Contextual sovereignty Each island keeps control of its own semantic universe.
Verifiable intersection The relationship is derived from a match, not from a central authority.
Structural privacy Only the space necessary to establish the connection is revealed.

The model shift

In a conventional network, two people meet because a server knows both of them. In this model, the connection emerges when their sets produce a valid intersection.
